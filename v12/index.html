Five features that keep customers coming back.

1. Today at a glance: progress on the home screen.
2. Live updates: follow a visit or session as it happens.
3. One-tap actions: check in without opening the app.
4. Gentle reminders: match the message to the moment.
5. Private by default: no sensitive details on a lock screen.

Which would your customers use most?

#healthtech #mobileapp #appdesign #digitalhealth

LinkedIn

The best moment in a health app is often the one people see without opening it.

Five ideas worth considering for your product:

1. Today at a glance. Progress on the home screen, so people don't have to hunt for it.
2. Live updates. Follow a visit or a session as it happens, right from the lock screen.
3. One-tap actions. Log a check-in without opening the app.
4. Gentle reminders. Match the message to the moment, so people don't switch notifications off.
5. Private by default. Show a calm summary on the lock screen and keep the details inside the app.

Apple's own design guidance recommends keeping sensitive details off screens other people can see, and says live updates are for things with a clear start and end, never for promotions.

We design and build mobile apps for health products. If you are thinking about which of these fit yours, let's talk.

Sources: Apple developer documentation and Human Interface Guidelines (widgets, Live Activities, notifications).

4 · Is your website fast enough?

Download LinkedIn PDF

Captions (Instagram and LinkedIn)

Instagram

Is your website fast enough?

Google checks three things:
- Does the main content show up in 2.5 seconds or less?
- Does the page react quickly when someone taps?
- Does the page hold still, without jumping around?

Three out of four visits should pass.

Curious about your own site? Let's take a look.

#webdesign #healthtech #digitalhealth #websitespeed

LinkedIn

Google measures how a website feels to the people visiting it, and it comes down to three questions.

- Does the page show up quickly? The main content should appear in 2.5 seconds or less.
- Does it answer straight away? The page should react within about a fifth of a second when someone taps.
- Does it hold still? Nothing should jump around while the page loads.

Google looks at real visits, and three out of four should pass, with phone and desktop checked separately.

An honest note on expectations: Google recommends good scores, but says a fast site does not guarantee a top ranking, and relevant content still comes first.

Sources: web.dev, Web Vitals; Google Search Central, page experience.

10 · Shipped last year doesn’t mean ready this year

Download LinkedIn PDF

Captions (Instagram and LinkedIn)

Instagram

Shipped last year doesn't mean ready this year.

Five places products age:
1. Speed
2. Phone updates
3. Key journeys
4. Reminders
5. Next steps

After every phone update, give your app three quick checks: open it on the newest software, check reminders and home-screen widgets, and check sign-in and permissions.

#digitalhealth #healthtech #webdesign #mobileapp

LinkedIn

A product that shipped last year is not automatically ready for this year. Five places worth a regular look:

1. Speed. Is the website fast enough? Google's targets: the main content in 2.5 seconds or less, a reaction within about a fifth of a second, and a page that holds still.
2. Phone updates. Open your app on the newest phone software and check reminders, home-screen widgets, sign-in and permissions.
3. Key journeys. Follow sign-up, intake or booking end to end and fix the step where most people stop.
4. Reminders. Is the message right for the moment, and private on a lock screen?
5. Next steps. Can people always find what to do next?

If you want a second pair of eyes on your product, we would be glad to take a look.

Sources: web.dev, Web Vitals; Apple Human Interface Guidelines.
